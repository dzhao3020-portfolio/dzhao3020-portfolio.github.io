Electronically actuated CVT

A CVT that shifts on command. I’m leading the design of an electronically actuated CVT for Northwestern Baja SAE, where a ball screw sets the primary sheave position.

Fixed constraints

  • The engine is the Kohler CH440 specified for Baja SAE, and its shaft must stay stock: 1 in. round with a standard keyway. Torque goes into the primary through a keyed plastic bushing.
  • The final drive ratio stays at 7.92:1.
  • The secondary’s maximum pitch diameter stays the same.

What’s open

Center distance was fixed at 8.5 in. and is now open to optimization, along with the primary’s maximum pitch diameter and belt length. I’m working through these with a belt and sheave kinematics model, and building a full SolidWorks assembly that moves the way the real mechanism will.

Actuation targets

A full shift from low to high should take 0.5 seconds. The calculated peak shift force is 892 lbf, delivered through a ball screw with a 5 mm lead. The actuator itself is fixed and can’t be resized, so the mechanism has to hit these targets within what it can deliver.
